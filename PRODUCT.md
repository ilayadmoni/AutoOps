# AutoOps Product

## Register

product

## Users

AutoOps serves system administrators and operations engineers managing RHEL fleets. They use it during focused operational work, often while validating machines, reviewing risk, approving commands, and monitoring live executions. The interface must keep critical state legible and make safe actions fast in both English and Hebrew.

## Product Purpose

AutoOps centralizes approved commands, typed workflows, SSH machine access, execution history, credentials, files, and AI-assisted proposals. Success means operators can understand what will run, trust the system's safeguards, and follow every operation from proposal through approval and execution without ambiguity.

## Brand Personality

Precise, dependable, and quietly technical. AutoOps should feel like a mature operations tool: focused, calm under pressure, and confident without decorative spectacle.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS dashboards, neon cyberpunk styling, decorative glass effects, oversized marketing treatments inside the product, and unfamiliar controls for standard tasks. Do not trade operational clarity for visual novelty.

## Design Principles

1. Make trust visible through clear state, approvals, security cues, and execution history.
2. Keep the operator in flow with familiar controls and concise feedback.
3. Treat English and Hebrew as equal, first-class interfaces with correct LTR and RTL behavior.
4. Use motion only to communicate progress, loading, and state changes.
5. Preserve a consistent component vocabulary across authentication and the app shell.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA contrast and interaction behavior. Every interactive control must be keyboard accessible with a visible focus state and useful accessible naming. Loading states must expose status semantics. Motion must respect reduced-motion preferences. Directionality must remain correct for Hebrew while technical values, credentials, paths, code, and addresses stay LTR.
